Five habits of clear writers

Clear writing isn't a talent. It's a handful of habits anyone can practise.

1. Say the point first

Readers decide in seconds whether to keep going. Put the most important sentence at the top, then explain it. Everything after the first paragraph is support.

2. Use short sentences

A sentence that carries one idea is easier to follow than one that carries three. When a sentence runs past two lines, look for the place to split it.

3. Prefer concrete words

"Sales fell 12% in March" beats "performance was suboptimal". Numbers, names and examples do more work than adjectives.

4. Cut what doesn't help

Read each paragraph and ask what the reader would lose without it. If the answer is nothing, delete it.

5. Read it out loud

Your ear catches what your eye skips: clumsy rhythm, repeated words, sentences that never land.
